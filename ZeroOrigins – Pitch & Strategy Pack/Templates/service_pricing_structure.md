## PART 1 — CHOOSING YOUR PRICING MODEL

| Model | Best For | Pros | Cons |
|-------|----------|------|------|
| Hourly | Variable-scope projects, ongoing support | Easy to start, transparent | Client-managed, hard to scale |
| Fixed / Project-Based | Defined deliverables, clear scope | Predictable, scalable | Risk if scope is unclear |
| Retainer | Ongoing relationships, maintenance, advisory | Recurring revenue, predictable | Requires consistent delivery each month |
| Productized Service | Repeated, packaged offer | Fastest to sell, easy to systemize | Less flexibility per client |
| Value-Based | High-stakes outcomes (revenue, growth) | Highest earning potential | Harder to justify without track record |

## PART 4 — RETAINER PRICING

**Retainers work best when:**
- Client needs ongoing support (not a one-time project)
- You can define a recurring deliverable or a set number of hours
- The relationship has already been established (usually after a project)

---

**Retainer Models:**

**Option A: Hours-based retainer**
> "[X] hours per month reserved for [CLIENT NAME] at a rate of [RATE]/hr.
> Hours do not roll over. Starts [DATE], billed on the 1st of each month."

Recommended for: advisory, fractional roles, ongoing support

**Option B: Deliverables-based retainer**
> "Monthly retainer includes: [LIST SPECIFIC MONTHLY DELIVERABLES].
> Any work outside this list is quoted separately."

Recommended for: content, social media, automation maintenance, SEO

**Option C: Access retainer**
> "Retainer provides priority access to [YOUR NAME] for questions,
> reviews, and guidance. Up to [X] async questions and [X] 30-minute
> calls per month."

Recommended for: consultants, coaches, advisors

## PART 5 — PRODUCTIZED SERVICE PRICING

A productized service is a fixed, packaged offering with a fixed price and defined scope.
It's the easiest thing to sell because clients know exactly what they're buying.

---

**How to productize:**

1. Identify your most repeatable service (what do you do the same way every time?)
2. Define exactly what's included
3. Set a fixed price and timeline
4. Give it a name

## PART 6 — PRICING PSYCHOLOGY TIPS

**Anchor high:**
Present your premium option first. It makes the middle option feel reasonable.

**Round numbers look guessed; specific numbers look calculated:**
$3,500 looks like a guess. $3,200 looks like you did the math.
(But don't be so specific it looks random: $3,187 raises eyebrows.)

**Bundle, don't itemize (for fixed pricing):**
Itemizing invites the client to pick and remove items. Present the package as a whole.

**Show the cost of NOT doing this:**
"Not having a clear scope costs you in revision time and client friction. This solves that."

**Never apologize for your price:**
State it once, clearly. Then stop talking. Let the client respond.

**Create urgency honestly:**
"I have [X] spots available this quarter." Only say this if it's true.
