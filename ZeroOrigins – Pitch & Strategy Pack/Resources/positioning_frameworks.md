# Positioning Frameworks

*How to explain your service so people immediately understand why they need it.*

---

## WHY POSITIONING MATTERS

Most freelancers and consultants lose deals not because their work isn't good,
but because they can't explain what they do in a way that lands.

"I do automation" doesn't land.
"I help service businesses stop losing leads because nobody followed up fast enough" lands.

The difference: one describes the tool, the other describes the outcome.

Positioning is the art of placing your service in the client's mind so clearly that
they immediately understand:
- Who you help
- What problem you fix
- Why you're the right person to fix it

Use the frameworks below to find your positioning.

---

## FRAMEWORK 1 — THE CLASSIC "I HELP X DO Y SO THEY CAN Z"

The most reliable structure. Simple, complete, memorable.

**Formula:**
> "I help [WHO] do [WHAT] so they can [WHY IT MATTERS]."

**Examples:**

> "I help interior design studios automate their lead response so they stop losing clients to competitors who replied faster."

> "I help SaaS founders build MVP prototypes in 2 weeks so they can test demand before spending on a full engineering team."

> "I help consultants structure their proposals so they close more projects without lowering their prices."

**How to fill it in:**

WHO: [Your most specific, narrow audience — not "anyone" or "businesses"]
WHAT: [The core action your service performs — specific]
WHY IT MATTERS: [The business outcome they care about — revenue, time, growth, peace of mind]

**Your version:**
> "I help __________ do __________ so they can __________."

---

## FRAMEWORK 2 — PROBLEM → SOLUTION → OUTCOME

A slightly longer version that works well in conversations and emails.

**Formula:**
> "A lot of [AUDIENCE] struggle with [PROBLEM]. I solve that by [SOLUTION]. The result is [MEASURABLE OUTCOME]."

**Examples:**

> "A lot of growing agencies struggle with scope creep and client disputes. I solve that by building them a client communication system — proposal, scope, and onboarding docs that set expectations before work begins. The result is fewer revision wars and more profitable projects."

> "A lot of solo consultants lose deals to agencies that just look more professional on paper. I solve that by building them a pitch kit — proposal templates, pricing structure, and a one-page offer — in a week. The result is deals they would have lost on positioning alone."

**Your version:**
> "A lot of __________ struggle with __________. I solve that by __________. The result is __________."

---

## FRAMEWORK 3 — THE BEFORE/AFTER BRIDGE

Powerful in proposals, pitch decks, and landing pages. Shows transformation clearly.

**Formula:**
Before → After → How I bridge it

**Example:**

> **Before:** You're manually following up with every lead. Some fall through the cracks. You lose 5–10 potential clients a month to faster competitors.
>
> **After:** Every inquiry triggers an instant, personalized response. Qualified leads book themselves into your calendar. You wake up with meetings already scheduled.
>
> **How I bridge it:** A done-for-you lead automation system built in 10 business days.

**Your version:**

> **Before:** [Current state — pain, manual work, inefficiency, lost opportunity]
>
> **After:** [Future state — automated, professional, efficient, profitable]
>
> **How I bridge it:** [Your service, stated simply]

---

## FRAMEWORK 4 — THE "WHAT, WHO, HOW" TRIFECTA

A three-part positioning statement that fits on one line.

**Formula:**
> "[WHAT] for [WHO] via [HOW]"

**Examples:**

> "Lead automation for service businesses via n8n workflows"

> "Client communication systems for consultants via proposal and scope document kits"

> "Brand positioning for B2B SaaS founders via messaging sprint"

This one is great for:
- Your LinkedIn headline
- Email signature
- First line of a pitch email
- Website tagline

**Your version:**
> "__________ for __________ via __________"

---

## FRAMEWORK 5 — THE CATEGORY OF ONE

Advanced positioning. Requires you to name what you do in a way no one else does.

Instead of: "I'm a freelance web designer"
You become: "I build conversion-focused landing pages for Indian DTC brands launching on Shopify"

The idea: be so specific that there's no one else in your exact category — which means no price comparison, no bidding war.

**How to build it:**

1. Choose a specific methodology or approach (not just a service)
2. Choose the most specific audience you can actually serve well
3. Combine them into a category label

**Examples:**

> "Proposal engineering for boutique agencies" — not "freelance writing"

> "AI automation onboarding for fintech startups" — not "automation consultant"

> "7-day brand sprint for direct-to-consumer founders" — not "branding"

**Your category:**
> "__________ for __________"

---

## FRAMEWORK 6 — THE ANALOGOUS POSITIONING

Use a well-known reference to make your positioning instantly clear.

**Formula:**
> "I'm like [KNOWN THING] but for [SPECIFIC AUDIENCE] / in [SPECIFIC CONTEXT]"

**Examples:**

> "Think of it as Calendly + CRM combined, but built specifically for coaching businesses."

> "This pack is like having a senior consultant walk you through your entire client communication process — in written form, on demand."

> "We're like an in-house automation team, but you don't pay salaries."

**Use with caution:** The comparison must be widely understood. Don't use references your client might not know.

---

## FRAMEWORK 7 — THE RESULT-FIRST POSITIONING

Lead with the result, follow with the method. Reverses the typical "here's what I do" approach.

**Formula:**
> "You'll [SPECIFIC RESULT] within [TIMEFRAME]. Here's how..."

**Examples:**

> "You'll have a fully functional lead response system running 24/7 within 10 business days. Here's what we build..."

> "Your next proposal will close at a higher rate because it shows the client you understood their problem before you pitched your solution. Here's what changes..."

This framing works because it leads with what the buyer cares about most.

## HOW TO TEST YOUR POSITIONING

### The 5-Second Test
Show your positioning statement to someone who doesn't know your industry.
Ask: "Do you understand what I do?"
If they say "sort of" or ask a follow-up question — rewrite it.

### The "So What?" Test
After every claim, ask: "So what?"
Keep asking until you can't go further. That's your real positioning.

> "I build automation systems." → So what?
> "Your team spends less time on manual tasks." → So what?
> "You can handle 3x the client volume without hiring." → That's it. Lead with that.

### The Differentiation Test
Ask: "Could my competitor say this exact thing?"
If yes, it's not your positioning — it's a category description. Keep going.

---

## CHOOSING YOUR POSITIONING

You don't need all of these frameworks. Pick ONE that feels most natural
for the way you talk about your work. Use it consistently:
- In emails
- On calls
- In proposals
- On your website
- On LinkedIn

Consistency builds recognition. Recognition builds trust. Trust closes deals.
