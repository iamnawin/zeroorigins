# Proposal Copy Examples

*Strong vs. weak writing — side by side.*
*Read this before writing your proposal.*
*The difference between a yes and a no is often just how something is written.*

---

## HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT

For each example:
1. Read the WEAK version first
2. Identify what's wrong with it
3. Read the STRONG version
4. Notice the specific difference

Then apply that principle to your own writing.

---

## EXAMPLE 1 — OPENING / INTRODUCTION

### WEAK

> "Thank you for considering my services. I am writing to provide you with a proposal for the work you described. I believe I can help you with your project and I look forward to working with you."

**What's wrong:**
- Could be sent to anyone. Zero personalization.
- Passive and uncertain ("I believe I can help")
- Tells them nothing about what comes next
- Reads like a job application from 2004

---

### STRONG

> "Hi Priya,
>
> Thanks for the call on Thursday — you gave me a clear picture of what's happening with Bloom Collective's lead pipeline and why fixing it before the Q3 push matters.
>
> This proposal outlines exactly how I'll build the system, what it will deliver, and what it costs. It's direct and specific because I think you've already done enough of the vague-conversations stage.
>
> Let me know if anything needs to be adjusted."

**Why it works:**
- Uses their name and references a real moment (Thursday's call)
- Shows they were heard: "Q3 push" is their priority, not a generic phrase
- Confident, not grovelling
- Sets a tone of mutual respect

## EXAMPLE 2 — UNDERSTANDING THEIR SITUATION

### WEAK

> "Your company needs to improve its online presence and increase brand awareness to attract more customers."

**What's wrong:**
- Could describe literally any company
- Vague: what kind of online presence? Which customers?
- Shows no understanding of their specific context
- Sounds like it was written without a discovery call

---

### STRONG

> "Right now, Bloom Collective receives around 40 inquiries per month through Instagram and your website form. Of those, roughly 25 get a response within 48 hours — and 15 wait 5+ days. Based on industry data, most premium buyers make a decision in the first 24 hours.
>
> What this means: you're losing an estimated 10–15 clients per month not because your product isn't strong, but because your response process can't keep up."

**Why it works:**
- Uses their actual numbers from the discovery call
- Frames the problem with consequence, not description
- No fluff — every sentence earns its place
- Makes them feel understood, not sold to

## EXAMPLE 3 — DESCRIBING A DELIVERABLE

### WEAK

> "I will build an automation system for your business."

**What's wrong:**
- Says nothing about what you're actually building
- Client has no idea what they're paying for
- No outcome, no specifics

---

### STRONG

> "**Automated Lead Response System:** An n8n workflow that triggers within 60 seconds of any new inquiry. The system sends a personalized WhatsApp message and an email with a direct booking link — automatically. No manual action required from you."

**Why it works:**
- Named and specific
- Time-bound ("within 60 seconds")
- Tells the client what it does AND what it means for them ("No manual action required")
- They can visualize it

---

## EXAMPLE 4 — PRICING PRESENTATION

### WEAK

> "The cost of this project will be Rs. 20,000. Please let me know if this is acceptable."

**What's wrong:**
- Invites negotiation from a weak position
- "Please let me know if this is acceptable" is apologetic
- No context for why it costs that
- No options — take it or leave it

---

### STRONG

> "**Option A — Core System:** ₹18,500
> Everything listed in Section 3 — complete automation build, testing, and a 45-minute walkthrough. Split 50/50.
>
> **Option B — System + 30-Day Support:** ₹22,000
> Everything in Option A, plus WhatsApp access and 3 minor tweaks during the first month. Most clients choose this for peace of mind while the system is new.
>
> Both options include 2 revision rounds. I'll send the invoice within an hour of your confirmation."

**Why it works:**
- Two options (not one, not five)
- The second option is positioned as the better choice without pressure
- Specific and confident — no "if this is acceptable"
- Tells them what happens next (invoice within an hour)

## EXAMPLE 5 — DESCRIBING YOUR TIMELINE

### WEAK

> "The project will take approximately 2–4 weeks depending on the complexity and your feedback."

**What's wrong:**
- Extremely vague ("2–4 weeks" is a huge range)
- Puts uncertainty and blame on the client ("your feedback")
- Sounds like a cover-your-ass statement, not a real plan

---

### STRONG

> "Here's how the project runs:
>
> - Days 1–3: System setup and connections
> - Days 4–7: Automation build and testing (15 scenarios)
> - Days 8–9: Your review round + my revisions
> - Day 10: Final delivery + walkthrough call
>
> **Total: 10 business days from deposit receipt.**
>
> *Note: Timeline assumes you provide Calendly access and email copy within 2 days of start. I'll flag if anything causes a shift before it becomes a problem.*"

**Why it works:**
- Specific phases, not a range
- Shows they've thought about what the client needs to do
- Proactive about potential delays — manages expectations without being defensive
- A client reading this knows exactly what to expect

## EXAMPLE 6 — THE CLOSING

### WEAK

> "I hope this proposal meets your requirements. Please feel free to reach out if you have any questions. I look forward to hearing from you."

**What's wrong:**
- "I hope" signals uncertainty
- "Please feel free" is filler
- Passive — doesn't tell them what to do
- Could be the end of any email ever written

---

### STRONG

> "This proposal is valid until 26 May. To move forward:
>
> 1. Reply with any questions (I'm on WhatsApp: +91 98765 43210)
> 2. Reply 'Let's do it' or sign the agreement (linked below)
> 3. I'll send the invoice within an hour
> 4. We start 20 May
>
> I think this system will pay for itself in the first month. Looking forward to building it with you."

**Why it works:**
- Clear numbered steps — zero ambiguity about what to do
- Phone number = accessibility and confidence
- Deadline creates honest urgency
- Ends with conviction ("pay for itself") not hope

## EXAMPLE 7 — "WHY ME" SECTION

### WEAK

> "I am a highly skilled professional with 5 years of experience in automation and AI. I am passionate about helping businesses grow and I always deliver high quality work on time."

**What's wrong:**
- Every freelancer says this
- "Passionate" is meaningless without evidence
- "High quality work on time" is the bare minimum — not a differentiator
- No specifics, no proof

---

### STRONG

> "I've built lead automation systems for service businesses specifically — a photography studio in Pune that went from 4-day response times to 8 minutes, and a brand consultancy in Bangalore that saw 40% more discovery calls booked in the first month.
>
> I don't do generic automation setups. Everything I build is specific to how your business actually works."

**Why it works:**
- Two specific client examples with specific results
- One-line positioning: "specific to how your business actually works" — this is a differentiator
- No filler adjectives

## GENERAL PRINCIPLES

**Be specific or be quiet.**
Vague language signals that you're guessing. Numbers, dates, names, and concrete outcomes build trust.

**Frame everything around their outcome, not your process.**
"I will build a Zapier workflow" tells them nothing they care about.
"You'll receive a notification in under 60 seconds when a lead comes in" tells them exactly why it matters.

**Confident writing wins more than humble writing.**
You can be warm and human while still being direct and clear about what you deliver and what it costs. Apologetic writing reads as insecure — and clients don't hire insecure service providers.

**Short beats long.**
If a sentence doesn't help them decide, cut it. A tight, sharp proposal wins over a comprehensive one every time.

**One ask at the end.**
Don't ask three questions in your closing. One. "Reply with any questions, or confirm and we'll get started."
