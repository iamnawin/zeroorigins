# Example Pitch Deck Structure

*A slide-by-slide outline for a service pitch deck.*
*Use this to build a deck in Canva, PowerPoint, Figma, Google Slides, or Notion.*

---

## What This Deck Is For

This structure is designed for:
- Agency or consultant pitching to a prospective client
- Freelancer presenting a service proposal in a meeting
- Founder pitching a service-based product to potential buyers
- Creator proposing a brand partnership or collaboration

**Ideal deck length:** 10–14 slides. More than 15 slides loses attention fast.

**Format principle:** One idea per slide. Less text, more clarity.

---

## SLIDE 1 — COVER

**What it shows:**
- Your name / company name
- The client's name (personalized)
- Project or pitch title
- Date

**Design tip:** Clean, minimal. Your logo + their name. This signals respect and preparation.

**Example headline:**
> "A Proposed Engagement for [CLIENT COMPANY]
> Prepared by [YOUR NAME / COMPANY] — [DATE]"

---

## SLIDE 2 — THE PROBLEM (THEIR WORLD)

*This is the most important slide in the deck. Get it right.*

**What it shows:**
- The specific pain, challenge, or gap the client is experiencing
- Evidence that you understand it (reference their situation, not a generic problem)
- The cost or consequence of not solving it

**Example text:**
> "Right now, [CLIENT COMPANY] is experiencing [PROBLEM].
> This is costing you [TIME / MONEY / OPPORTUNITY].
> And it's getting worse as [RELEVANT TREND]."

**Design tip:** Bold, single-focus. One problem per slide.

---

## SLIDE 3 — WHY THIS MATTERS NOW

**What it shows:**
- The urgency factor — why now, not later
- Market forces, timing, competition, internal deadlines

**Example text:**
> "With [TREND / EVENT / DEADLINE], the window to fix this is narrow.
> Acting now means [ADVANTAGE]. Waiting means [COST]."

**Design tip:** Use a simple stat or timeline visual if you have one.

---

## SLIDE 4 — THE SOLUTION (HIGH-LEVEL)

**What it shows:**
- Your approach to solving their problem
- The methodology or framework you use
- Why your solution is the right one for their situation

**Example text:**
> "We solve this through [3-STEP APPROACH / METHOD NAME].
> Here's what that looks like for [CLIENT COMPANY]..."

**Design tip:** A simple 3-column or icon-based layout works well here.

---

## SLIDE 5 — WHAT WE'LL DELIVER

**What it shows:**
- Specific deliverables
- Outcomes the client will have at the end

**Format:**
| # | Deliverable | What It Means For You |
|---|-------------|----------------------|
| 1 | [ITEM] | [BENEFIT] |
| 2 | [ITEM] | [BENEFIT] |
| 3 | [ITEM] | [BENEFIT] |

**Design tip:** Frame every deliverable in terms of benefit, not just description.

---

## SLIDE 6 — HOW IT WORKS (PROCESS)

**What it shows:**
- Phase-by-phase breakdown of how the project will run
- Timeline overview
- Your workflow / how you operate

**Format:**
- Phase 1: [NAME] — [Brief description] — Week 1–2
- Phase 2: [NAME] — [Brief description] — Week 3–4
- Phase 3: [NAME] — [Brief description] — Week 5

**Design tip:** A horizontal timeline visual or numbered flow works well.

---

## SLIDE 7 — THE OUTCOME / TRANSFORMATION

**What it shows:**
- Before and after — where they are now vs. where they'll be
- The business impact of this project
- The "so what" of everything above

**Format:**

| Before | After |
|--------|-------|
| [CURRENT STATE — PAIN] | [FUTURE STATE — GAIN] |
| [CURRENT STATE] | [FUTURE STATE] |

**Example headline:**
> "Here's what changes for [CLIENT COMPANY] when this is done."

**Design tip:** High contrast, simple visual. This is the emotional core of the deck.

---

## SLIDE 8 — WHY US

*Keep this short. Credentials are context, not the pitch.*

**What it shows:**
- Why you are the right person/team for this project
- Relevant experience or past results (be specific, not vague)
- One or two social proof signals: testimonial, case study mention, client logo

**Example:**
> "I've helped [TYPE OF CLIENT] achieve [SPECIFIC RESULT].
> Here's what a recent client said: '[QUOTE]'."

**Design tip:** One quote, one relevant result. Don't overload.

---

## SLIDE 9 — INVESTMENT

**What it shows:**
- Pricing options (2–3 tiers ideally)
- What's included in each
- Payment terms at a glance

**Format:**

| Starter | Standard | Premium |
|---------|----------|---------|
| [PRICE] | [PRICE] | [PRICE] |
| [WHAT'S IN] | [WHAT'S IN] | [WHAT'S IN] |
| [WHAT'S IN] | [WHAT'S IN] | [WHAT'S IN] |

**Design tip:** Highlight the middle option. Use a subtle "Recommended" badge.

---

## SLIDE 10 — TIMELINE

**What it shows:**
- Proposed start date
- Key milestones
- Expected delivery date

**Example:**
> "With a confirmed start on [DATE], here's how this plays out..."

A simple Gantt bar or milestone list works well here.

---

## SLIDE 11 — HOW TO GET STARTED

**What it shows:**
- The exact next step (one action only)
- Contact information
- Deadline for this offer (creates urgency)

**Example:**
> "To move forward:
> 1. Reply to this deck with any questions
> 2. Sign the proposal doc (link below)
> 3. Submit deposit to begin
>
> This proposal is valid until [DATE]."

**Design tip:** Big CTA button or highlighted contact info. Make it easy to act.

---

## SLIDE 12 — APPENDIX (OPTIONAL)

Include only if the client needs more technical detail or reassurance.

- Case study or past project breakdown
- FAQs
- Detailed methodology
- Team bios
- References

**When to include:** Long deals, enterprise clients, complex technical projects.
**When to skip:** Most standard client pitches — keep it lean.

---

## TIPS FOR DELIVERING THIS DECK

**In person or on a call:**
- Talk to the slide, not at it. Use the visual as a prompt, not a script.
- Pause on Slide 2 (The Problem). Ask: "Does this match how you'd describe it?"
- Pause on Slide 9 (Investment). Don't explain the price — let them react first.

**Sent asynchronously (PDF or link):**
- Add speaker notes in the presenter view or a brief intro email
- Include a short Loom walkthrough video for high-value deals
- End your email with a single question: "Any questions before you decide?"

**One thing to never do:**
Read the slides word-for-word. If your slides can replace you, your slides are wrong.
