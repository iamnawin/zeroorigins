# ZO-SALES-002 — Discovery Call SOP

---

## 1. Purpose

To run a structured 30-minute discovery call that qualifies the client, uncovers their real problem, and creates the foundation for a proposal. A great discovery call does most of the selling — the proposal just confirms what was already agreed.

---

## 2. Scope

All first calls with potential clients for ZeroOrigins automation services, AI systems, or workshops.

---

## 3. Owner

Founder

---

## 4. Trigger

After a lead is captured and expresses interest in a call.

---

## 5. Inputs Required

- Lead's name, company, and what they said they need (from Lead Tracker)
- Client Discovery Form (sent before the call — see `09_Templates/Client_Discovery_Form_Template.md`)
- Your Calendly link set to 30 minutes
- Notes template open and ready

---

## 6. Before the Call

**24 hours before:**
- [ ] Send the discovery form via email: "Hi [Name], looking forward to our call. Could you fill in this quick form before we meet? It helps me prepare so we make the best use of our 30 mins. [link]"
- [ ] Research the company (2–3 minutes): website, LinkedIn, size, industry
- [ ] Review their form responses if submitted

**5 minutes before:**
- [ ] Open the discovery call notes template
- [ ] Test your mic, camera, and internet
- [ ] Be in a quiet, professional space

---

## 7. Discovery Call Structure (30 Minutes)

### 0–2 min: Opening

"Thanks for making time, [Name]. Quick plan for today — I'll start by understanding your business and what you're trying to solve. Then I'll share a bit about how we work and what might be relevant. And we'll end with whether there's a fit and what the next step is. Sound good?"

*This sets expectations and gives you control of the call.*

---

### 2–10 min: Understand Their World

Ask open-ended questions. Listen 80%, talk 20%.

**Business context:**
- "Tell me a bit about what your business does and who your customers are."
- "How big is your team right now?"
- "What tools are you currently using to run your operations?"

**The pain:**
- "What's the biggest manual or repetitive task eating your time or your team's time right now?"
- "Walk me through how [process] works today, step by step."
- "What breaks most often in your current setup?"

**The cost of the problem:**
- "How much time per week does your team spend on this?"
- "Has it caused you to miss leads, delay responses, or lose clients?"
- "If we solved this completely, what would that free up for you?"

*Listen carefully. Write down exact words they use — these become your proposal language.*

---

### 10–18 min: Qualify

**Budget:**
- "Have you invested in tools or automation before, or is this new territory for you?"
- "Do you have a rough sense of what budget you're working with for a solution like this?"
- *If they resist: "I ask only because I want to make sure I recommend the right scope — our projects start at Rs. 15,000 and go up depending on complexity."*

**Decision process:**
- "If we identify the right solution today, what would the approval process look like on your end?"
- "Is anyone else involved in this decision?"

**Timeline:**
- "Is there a deadline or event driving this? Or is this something you'd like to solve in the next 30–60 days?"

---

### 18–25 min: Share Relevant Context

Only share what's relevant to what they told you. Do not pitch everything.

"Based on what you've shared, this sounds like a [lead capture / client onboarding / internal alerting] automation challenge. We've built systems exactly like this for [type of client]. Here's broadly what we'd do..."

Give a 2–3 sentence description of the solution. Keep it outcome-focused:
"We'd connect your [form/CRM/spreadsheet] to your [email/WhatsApp], score leads automatically, and set up follow-up sequences — so your team only talks to warm leads."

Ask: "Does that sound like what you need?"

---

### 25–30 min: Close and Next Step

**If there's a fit:**
"Great — what I'd like to do is put together a specific proposal for you. It'll cover the scope, timeline, and pricing. I can have it to you by [day]. Does that work?"

**If unclear fit:**
"I'd like to think about this a bit more and come back to you with what I'd recommend. Can I send you a short proposal by [day]?"

**If no fit:**
"Based on what you've shared, I don't think we're the right fit for this particular need. But I'd be happy to point you toward someone who might be."

---

### After Every Call (Within 1 Hour)

- [ ] Update Lead Tracker: status → Proposal Pending / Not Qualified / Nurture
- [ ] Write 5–10 bullet point notes from the call
- [ ] Send follow-up email within 2 hours:

**Subject:** "Great talking with you, [Name] — next steps"

"Hi [Name], thanks for the call. Here's a quick summary of what we discussed:

- [Their problem — in their words]
- [What we'd solve]
- [Next step: proposal by [date] / more info needed]

I'll [send proposal / be in touch by [date]].

Let me know if anything changes in the meantime."

## 12. Risks / Mistakes to Avoid

- **Pitching too early** — let them talk first. If you pitch in the first 10 minutes, you're guessing at the problem.
- **Not asking about budget** — if they can't afford you, know early. It saves both of you time.
- **Not confirming next step before hanging up** — "I'll send something later" ends deals. Confirm date, format, and what happens next before you end the call.
- **Not sending follow-up email** — the follow-up email keeps you top of mind and shows professionalism.
- **Not updating Lead Tracker** — one call is worthless without tracking.
